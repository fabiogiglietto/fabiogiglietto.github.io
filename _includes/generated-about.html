Fabio Giglietto is Full Professor of Internet Studies at the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo, where he teaches Generative AI and Media and Digital Social Network Analysis. His research sits at the intersection of computational social science, political communication, and the analysis of digital platforms, with a primary focus on information disorders and coordinated social media behavior. He is also a founding partner (socio fondatore) of Digit Srl, a benefit-corporation spin-off of the University of Urbino that designs digital platforms dedicated to sustainability, civic participation, social innovation, and scientific dissemination.

Since 2017, he has coordinated the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE), which serves as the institutional umbrella for his ongoing research line. MINE has hosted a succession of externally funded sub-projects investigating the impact of social media on elections, public opinion, and health information. While these external grants have concluded—including his recent roles as WP4 Leader on the Horizon Europe project vera.ai (concluded 2025) and Partner on the PROMPT project (concluded 2026)—the programme remains active. His recent work on platform policies, such as his 2025 study on Meta’s political content reduction, has attracted significant policy interest and was cited in a written question to the European Commission.

Within MINE, Giglietto co-developed CooRnet, an open-source R package that pioneered the Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) detection method used globally by researchers and fact-checkers. Following the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle in August 2024, CooRnet was operationally discontinued. However, the CLSB methodology lives on through CooRTweet, an independent, actively maintained downstream package developed by other researchers to detect coordinated behavior across various platforms. His research has been covered by major national outlets, including Il Sole 24 ORE, Il Resto del Carlino, and Open.

Beyond his research and teaching, Giglietto is actively involved in the international academic community. He is a member of the International Communication Association (ICA), the Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR), and the Italian Association for Political Communication. Additionally, he has contributed to the governance of his field by serving on the board of Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics within the International Sociological Association (ISA RC51). Through these networks, he continues to foster collaborative approaches to understanding the evolving dynamics of social media governance, algorithmic influence, and digital public spaces.

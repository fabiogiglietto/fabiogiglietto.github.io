Fabio Giglietto is a Full Professor of Internet Studies at the University of Urbino Carlo Bo, where he also earned his doctorate. His academic work explores the intersections of digital platforms, political communication, and computational social science, with a particular focus on coordinated online behavior and information disorders. In the classroom, he instructs students in subjects such as Generative AI and Media alongside Digital Social Network Analysis. Deepening his commitment to applying academic research to societal needs, he is a founding partner of Digit Srl, a university spin-off and benefit corporation that designs digital systems to support sustainability, social innovation, civic engagement, and public science communication.

To anchor his empirical research, he established the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE) at the University of Urbino. While all external grants under this initiative have concluded, MINE remains the central umbrella for his scholarly endeavors. Within this framework, he co-created CooRnet, an open-source R package designed to detect coordinated link-sharing behavior. Following the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle in late 2024, active development on CooRnet ceased, though its underlying methodology continues to influence independent downstream tools like CooRTweet, which is maintained by other scholars.

His expertise has led to significant contributions within European research consortia and regulatory bodies. He recently served as a Work Package Leader for the Horizon Europe project vera.ai and as a partner in the PROMPT initiative, both focusing on countering disinformation and tracing manipulative narratives. Additionally, he has supported the European Commission as an independent evaluator for the European Education and Culture Executive Agency and contributed scientific oversight to the Joint Research Centre's expert group on systemic risks for online platforms. His recent research examining Meta's political content reduction policies has attracted national press attention from outlets such as Il Sole 24 Ore and Open, and was cited in a written inquiry to the European Parliament.

He maintains active connections with the global research community through memberships in the International Communication Association, the Association of Internet Researchers, and the Italian Association for Political Communication, and has served on the board of the International Sociological Association's Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics. His recent scholarly publications examine how algorithmic governance, partisan alignment, and generative artificial intelligence alter the distribution of information on social media. By analyzing deceptive information campaigns and platform data access challenges under the Digital Services Act, his work continues to map the evolving dynamics of the digital public sphere.

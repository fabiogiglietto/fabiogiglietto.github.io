Fabio Giglietto is Full Professor of Internet Studies in the Department of Communication Sciences, Humanities and International Studies at the University of Urbino Carlo Bo, where he also earned his doctorate. His research sits at the intersection of computational social science, the analysis of digital platforms and political communication, with a particular focus on information disorders and coordinated behaviour on social media. His teaching at Urbino has included courses on Generative AI and Media and Digital Social Network Analysis.

In 2017 he founded the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE) at Urbino and has coordinated it since. MINE investigates Italian media coverage and the impact of social media on elections, public opinion and health information, and has hosted a succession of externally funded sub-projects. Within the programme he co-developed CooRnet, with Nicola Righetti and Luca Rossi: an open-source R package that introduced the Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior detection method and was applied internationally by researchers, journalists and fact-checkers. Operational development of CooRnet ended after Meta shut down CrowdTangle, on which the package relied, in August 2024; its methodology continues to inform downstream tools such as CooRTweet, an independent package developed by Nicola Righetti and Paul Balluff.

His work connects closely with European digital policy. He served as Work Package 4 Leader of the Horizon Europe project vera.ai, which developed AI-based tools for countering disinformation, and as a partner on PROMPT, a European Commission-funded project monitoring disinformation narratives in Europe. As an independent expert he was a member of a Joint Research Centre expert group on systemic risks of very large online platforms, a study contributing to the European Centre for Algorithmic Transparency under Article 40(12) of the Digital Services Act, in which he provided scientific oversight, monitored the platform data-access request process and authored the group's summary reports. He was also appointed evaluator and rapporteur for the European Education and Culture Executive Agency, assessing project proposals.

His 2025 working paper on Meta's political content reduction policy and the Facebook visibility of Italian parliamentarians was cited in a written question to the European Commission and drew national media coverage in Italy, including Il Sole 24 Ore, Il Resto del Carlino, Open and Lumsanews. Giglietto is a member of the International Communication Association, the Association of Internet Researchers and the Italian Association for Political Communication, and has served on the board of the International Sociological Association's Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics. He is a founding partner of Digit Srl, a benefit-corporation spin-off of the University of Urbino that develops digital platforms for sustainability, civic participation, social innovation and scientific dissemination.

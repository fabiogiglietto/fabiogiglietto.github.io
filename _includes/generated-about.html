Fabio Giglietto is Full Professor of Internet Studies at the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo, where he teaches Generative AI and Media and Digital Social Network Analysis. His research lies at the intersection of computational social science, digital platform analysis, and political communication, with a particular focus on information disorders and coordinated behavior on social media. He is the founder and coordinator of the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE), active since 2017. Serving as the institutional umbrella for his ongoing research, MINE has hosted a succession of externally funded sub-projects examining media coverage and the impact of social networks on elections, public opinion, and health information. Although these external grants have concluded, the program remains the core vehicle for his academic investigations.

Within the MINE program, Giglietto co-developed CooRnet, an open-source R package that introduced the Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) detection methodology to help researchers and journalists surface coordinated social media activity. Following the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle in August 2024, CooRnet was operationally discontinued, though its foundational CLSB methodology persists through CooRTweet, an independent downstream package maintained by other researchers. On the European stage, Giglietto has contributed to major collaborative initiatives, serving as WP4 Leader for the Horizon Europe project vera.ai, which concluded in 2025, and as a Partner on the PROMPT project, which concluded in 2026. These projects focused respectively on developing artificial intelligence tools to counter disinformation and tracking the propagation of manipulative narratives across Europe.

His recent research includes a 2025 empirical audit of Meta's political content reduction policy and its impact on the visibility of Italian parliamentarians. This work was cited in a written question to the European Commission and received national media coverage in early 2026 across outlets such as Il Sole 24 Ore, Il Resto del Carlino, Open, and Lumsanews. Beyond academia, Giglietto is a founding partner of Digit Srl, a benefit-corporation spin-off of the University of Urbino Carlo Bo that designs digital platforms for civic participation, sustainability, and scientific dissemination. He is also an active member of the International Communication Association, the Association of Internet Researchers, and the Italian Association for Political Communication, and has served on the board of the International Sociological Association's Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics.

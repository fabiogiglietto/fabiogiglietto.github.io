Fabio Giglietto is a Full Professor of Internet Studies at the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo, where he also earned his doctorate. His research operates at the intersection of computational social science, digital platform analysis, and political communication, with a particular focus on information disorders and coordinated social media behavior. In the classroom, he shares his expertise by teaching courses on Generative AI and Media as well as Digital Social Network Analysis. Through these academic endeavors, he explores how digital platforms shape modern society, public discourse, and the flow of information.

He is the founder and coordinator of the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE), an institutional research umbrella active since 2017. Under this framework, Giglietto has led a succession of externally funded sub-projects on elections, public opinion, and health information. Though all external grants have concluded, MINE continues to serve as the core hub for his ongoing research. His recent European contributions include serving as the Work Package 4 Leader for the Horizon Europe project vera.ai, which concluded in late 2025, and as a partner on the PROMPT project, which concluded in early 2026, both of which focused on countering disinformation and monitoring problematic online narratives.

As part of his methodological contributions, Giglietto co-developed CooRnet, an open-source R package that pioneered the Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior detection method used globally by researchers and investigative journalists. Following the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle in August 2024, CooRnet was formally discontinued at the operational level. However, the foundational methodology it introduced lives on in CooRTweet, an independent, downstream R package maintained by other scholars to detect coordinated behavior across modern platform APIs.

Beyond his primary research and teaching, Giglietto is highly active in the international academic community. He holds memberships in the International Communication Association, the Association of Internet Researchers, and the Italian Association for Political Communication, and has previously served on the board of the International Sociological Association's Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics. He is also a founding partner of Digit Srl, a benefit-corporation spin-off of the University of Urbino Carlo Bo. This venture focuses on developing innovative digital platforms dedicated to sustainability, civic participation, social innovation, and the broad dissemination of scientific knowledge.

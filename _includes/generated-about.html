Fabio Giglietto is a Full Professor of Internet Studies at the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo, where he also completed his doctoral studies. At the university, he currently teaches courses in Generative AI and Media alongside Digital Social Network Analysis. Situated at the intersection of computational social science, digital platform analysis, and political communication, his research primarily investigates information disorders and coordinated behavior across social media platforms. Over his career, his work has examined how digital infrastructures and algorithmic systems shape public discourse. He also contributes to the local innovation ecosystem as a founding partner (socio fondatore) of Digit Srl, a university benefit-corporation spin-off that builds digital platforms dedicated to civic participation, sustainability, social innovation, and scientific dissemination.

Since 2017, he has coordinated the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE), an institutional umbrella hosting a succession of externally funded sub-projects exploring elections, public opinion, and health information. Although its external grants have concluded, MINE continues to guide his current research agenda. Under this program, he co-developed CooRnet, an open-source R package that pioneered the Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) detection methodology used worldwide by researchers and fact-checkers. Following the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle in August 2024, CooRnet was discontinued at the operational level. However, the CLSB methodology remains active through CooRTweet, an independent downstream R package maintained by other researchers.

On the European stage, he has contributed to major initiatives countering digital manipulation, serving as a work package leader for the vera.ai project and as a partner on the PROMPT initiative. He also serves as an independent expert for the European Commission, notably contributing scientific oversight to the Joint Research Centre's group on systemic risks of very large online platforms under the Digital Services Act, and evaluating proposals for the European Education and Culture Executive Agency. His recent research includes a 2025 analysis of Meta's political content reduction policy, which was cited in a 2026 European Parliament written question and received national media coverage from Italian outlets such as Il Sole 24 Ore, Il Resto del Carlino, Open, and Lumsanews.

Giglietto is an active member of several international academic communities, including the International Communication Association (ICA), the Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR), and the Italian Association for Political Communication, and has served on the board of the International Sociological Association's Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics. His recent scholarly contributions continue to explore the evolving landscape of platform governance, deceptive information operations, and the methodologies required to study them. By bridging academic inquiry with public policy and practical digital tools, his research addresses the critical challenges of maintaining scientific data access and understanding the societal impacts of algorithmic curation in a rapidly changing digital environment.

Fabio Giglietto is Full Professor of Internet Studies at the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo, where he teaches Generative AI and Media alongside Digital Social Network Analysis. His research lies at the intersection of computational social science, digital platform analysis, and political communication, with a particular focus on information disorders and coordinated behavior on social media. Since 2017, he has coordinated the Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE), which serves as the institutional umbrella for his research line. MINE has hosted a succession of externally funded sub-projects examining elections, public opinion, and health information. Although all its external grants have concluded, MINE remains the active framework for his ongoing scholarly inquiries.

As part of his methodological contributions, Giglietto co-developed CooRnet, an open-source R package that pioneered the detection of Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB). This methodology has been widely adopted by researchers and fact-checkers globally. Following the shutdown of Meta’s CrowdTangle on August 14, 2024, CooRnet was discontinued at the operational level. However, its core CLSB methodology continues to thrive through downstream tools, most notably the independent R package CooRTweet, which is maintained by other researchers to detect coordinated behavior across modern platform APIs.

On the European stage, Giglietto recently served as the WP4 Leader for the Horizon Europe project vera.ai (concluded in October 2025) and as a partner on the PROMPT initiative (concluded in February 2026), both dedicated to countering disinformation and tracking problematic narratives. His recent research into Meta's recommendation algorithms and their impact on democratic processes garnered significant public attention in early 2026. This work, which highlighted how algorithmic shifts affect the visibility of political content, was featured on Rai 3's investigative program Report and sparked policy discussions at both national and European levels.

Beyond his academic research and teaching, Giglietto is highly active in the international scientific community. He is a member of the International Communication Association (ICA), the Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR), the Italian Association for Political Communication, and has served on the board of the International Sociological Association's Research Committee 51 (Sociocybernetics). He is also a founding partner (socio fondatore) of Digit Srl, a benefit-corporation spin-off of the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo. This enterprise focuses on developing digital platforms that foster sustainability, civic participation, social innovation, and the dissemination of scientific knowledge.

# Fabio Giglietto — Fact Sheet

*Status information in this document is stamped "as of August 2026". All other statements refer to completed events.*

## Profile

As of August 2026, Fabio Giglietto is Full Professor of Internet Studies at the Università di Urbino Carlo Bo, where his teaching has included Generative AI and Media and Digital Social Network Analysis. He earned his doctorate at the same institution. His research sits at the intersection of computational social science, analysis of digital platforms and political communication, with a specific focus on information disorders and coordinated behaviour on social media.

## Research programme

In 2017 he founded the **Mapping Italian News Research Program (MINE)** — <https://mine.uniurb.it/> — at the University of Urbino, and has coordinated it since. MINE investigates Italian media coverage and the impact of social media on electoral processes, public opinion and health information. Between 2017 and February 2026 it hosted a succession of externally funded sub-projects supported by the Open Society Foundations, the Social Science Research Council (with Facebook data via Social Science One), the Italian MUR, the Gates Foundation, the Media Authority of North Rhine-Westphalia, and the European Commission (see the table below); all of those sub-projects had concluded by February 2026. As of August 2026 the MINE programme remains active as the institutional umbrella for his research line.

Within MINE, Giglietto co-developed **CooRnet** (with Nicola Righetti and Luca Rossi), the open-source R package that introduced the *Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior* (CLSB) detection method, applied internationally by researchers, journalists and fact-checkers. Development of CooRnet ended at the operational level after the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle on 14 August 2024, on which the package depended for data access.

## EU-funded research projects

He served as **WP4 Leader** of the Horizon Europe project **vera.ai** (concluded October 2025), which developed AI-based tools for countering disinformation, and as Partner on **PROMPT** (funded by the European Commission, DG CNECT; concluded February 2026), dedicated to monitoring disinformation narratives in Europe. Both were competitively awarded grants in which the University of Urbino was a beneficiary; see the projects table below.

## Service to the European Commission as an independent expert

He was a member of the **Joint Research Centre** expert group on *Systemic Risks of Very Large Online Platforms with Publicly Available Data* (Scope ID 11824), a study contributing to the **European Centre for Algorithmic Transparency (ECAT)** under **Article 40(12) of the Digital Services Act**. In that role he provided scientific oversight of the group's research projects, monitored the platform data-access request process and authored its summary reports, delivered in 2026.

He was appointed **evaluator and rapporteur for the European Education and Culture Executive Agency (EACEA)**, assessing project proposals; as of August 2026 that appointment ran to September 2026.

## Impact of the 2025 Meta working paper

His 2025 working paper on Meta's political content reduction policy (DOI 10.31235/osf.io/8dqag) was **cited in a written question to the European Commission** (document E-10-2026-000086, European Parliament, 2026) — <https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/E-10-2026-000086_IT.html>. Between January and April 2026 it also drew **national media coverage** in Italy, including *Il Sole 24 Ore*, *Il Resto del Carlino*, *Open* and *Lumsanews*.

## Professional affiliations

As of August 2026 he is a member of the International Communication Association (ICA), the Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR) and the Italian Association for Political Communication. He has served on the board of Research Committee 51 on Sociocybernetics of the International Sociological Association. He is a **founding partner (socio fondatore) of Digit Srl** (<https://digit.srl/>), a benefit-corporation spin-off of the University of Urbino Carlo Bo that develops digital platforms for sustainability, civic participation, social innovation and scientific dissemination.

## Software tools and resources developed

- **CooRnet** (R package; Giglietto, Righetti & Rossi, 2020): introduced the Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) detection method on Facebook/Instagram and was adopted in international research and investigative reports. Development ended at the operational level after the shutdown of Meta's CrowdTangle on 14 August 2024, on which the package relied for data access. The CLSB methodology it introduced continued to inform downstream tools; CooRnet is cited in Bruns et al. (2025).
- **CooRTweet** (Righetti & Balluff, first released 2023): an independent R package, developed by Nicola Righetti (Uniurb DISCUI) and Paul Balluff (GESIS), that builds on CooRnet's CLSB methodology to provide platform- and content-agnostic detection of coordinated behaviour on social media. It does not depend on CrowdTangle; version 2.1.3, released on CRAN in March 2025, supported Twitter/X and Meta Content Library data. Methodological paper: Righetti & Balluff (2025), *Computational Communication Research* 7(1). Giglietto is not a co-developer.
